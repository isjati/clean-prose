# Scoring rubric and quick checks

## Score (1 to 10 each, total /50)

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Directness | Does it make statements, or announce them? |
| Rhythm | Do sentence lengths and paragraph endings vary, or is it metronomic? |
| Trust | Does it respect the reader's intelligence, without hand-holding or over-justifying? |
| Authenticity | Does it sound like a specific person wrote it? |
| Density | Is anything cuttable? (10 = nothing to cut) |

Below 35: revise and score again. Report the score as one line per dimension plus the three highest-impact fixes.

## Quick checks before delivering

- Throat-clearing ("Here's what / here's why / the truth is")? Cut to the point.
- "Not X, it's Y"? State Y.
- Three consecutive sentences of the same length? Break one.
- Every paragraph ending on a punchy one-liner? Vary them.
- Vague declarative ("The implications are significant", "The reasons are structural")? Name the implication or the reason.
- Meta-joiners ("The rest of this essay...", "In this section, we'll...")? Delete.
- Lazy extremes (every, always, never, nobody) doing vague work? Replace with the specific scope.
- Inanimate thing doing a human verb? Name the person (see below).

## False agency

Things do not act; people do. Name the actor, or use "you" when no specific person fits.

| Avoid | Problem |
|---|---|
| "the complaint became a fix" | Someone fixed it |
| "the decision emerged" | Someone decided |
| "the culture shifted" | People changed what they do |
| "the data tells us" | Someone read the data and concluded |
| "the market rewards" | Buyers pay for things |

Technical systems may act ("the scheduler retries the job", "Docker restarts the container"). That is accurate, not false agency.

## Business jargon

| Avoid | Use |
|---|---|
| navigate (challenges) | handle, deal with |
| unpack | explain |
| lean into | accept, commit to |
| landscape | situation, field, market |
| double down | commit further |
| deep dive | analysis, close look |
| circle back | return to |
| moving forward / going forward | next, from now on |
| on the same page | agreed |

## Emphasis crutches and meta-commentary

Delete: "Full stop.", "Period.", "Let that sink in.", "Make no mistake", "Here's why that matters", "Plot twist:", "Spoiler:", "Hint:", "You already know this, but", "X is a feature, not a bug", "Let me walk you through", "As we'll see".
