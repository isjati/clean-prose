# Clean prose: drafting rules

Apply these while writing anything a person will read: documents, notes, slide copy, speaker notes, commit and PR text, and bot or chat replies. They are cheap defaults. For a full rewrite, audit or score, use the `clean-prose` skill.

1. Say the point directly. Do not use "not X but Y", "it's not just X, it's Y" or "X rather than Y" unless the negative half corrects a belief the reader actually holds.
2. Cut staged openers ("Let's dive in", "Here's the thing", "Honestly?") and one-line closers that restate the paragraph ("That's the real win", "Let that sink in").
3. Do not inflate ordinary facts. Avoid pivotal, testament, landscape, delve, leverage, robust (figurative), seamless, game-changer, "plays a key role", "marks a turning point", and trailing "-ing" riders such as "highlighting" or "underscoring".
4. Use as many items as the content has. Do not force lists of three.
5. Use "is", "are" and "has" instead of "serves as", "stands as", "boasts" or "features".
6. Em dashes: none in short copy, at most one or two in a long document.
7. Bold only what carries meaning. No emoji or arrows as decoration in headings. Headings in sentence case.
8. No chatbot residue inside deliverables: "I hope this helps", "Great question", "Let me know if...".
9. Never add a fact, number, name, date, quote or source that is not in the input or the conversation. Ask instead.
10. Indonesian follows the same rules. Avoid "tidak hanya ... tetapi juga" used as filler, "di era digital ini", "memainkan peran penting", "sangat krusial", "Mari kita bahas", "Semoga bermanfaat". Passive "di-" forms are normal Indonesian and are not a tell.

Scope: these rules do not apply to code, commands, logs, quoted text, data, proper names or titles. In chat replies, normal Markdown structure (headings, tables, bold labels for scanning) is fine; the wording rules still apply.
